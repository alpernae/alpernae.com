## The Beginning: The Spark of Curiosity

My name is Alperen, and I'm 22 years old. From a young age, I've always been curious about technology. However, my interest in cybersecurity truly started when I was around 10 or 11 years old, in the fifth grade. At that time, the mysterious world of computers and the internet fascinated me. But it was only curiosity — I hadn't taken any real steps to pursue it yet. That is, until a significant event gave me the push I needed to dive into the world of cybersecurity.

During this period, a hacker group called Turkhackteam carried out a cyber attack that made headlines across Turkey. The power and impact of this attack opened my eyes to the importance of cybersecurity, and I realized I wanted to explore this field further. After doing some research, I found the forum for this group and joined it. This was my first real step into the world of cybersecurity.

## First Steps: Gaining Knowledge and Practicing Skills

The forum was a goldmine for someone like me, eager to learn. Back then, it had a reputation for being involved in some illegal activities, but my focus was on learning the basics of cybersecurity. The articles and guides I found on the forum helped me build a theoretical foundation. As I accumulated more knowledge, I felt the urge to put what I had learned into practice.

Soon enough, I began trying my hand at hacking, using what I had learned from the forum. It wasn't just about testing my skills — I was also building a reputation within the community. During this time, I primarily focused on SQL Injection vulnerabilities, regularly exploiting websites and leaking their databases. But I eventually realized that I was limiting myself. Instead of gaining a comprehensive understanding of cybersecurity, I had fixated on one type of vulnerability.

## A Shift in Focus: Reassessing My Goals

As I approached the end of middle school, I had to take a step back from cybersecurity. The high school entrance exam was looming, and I needed to prepare. While studying for the exam, I also spent a lot of time thinking about my future career. At that time, cybersecurity was starting to gain recognition in Turkey, but there were very few formal educational programs for it.

When it came time to choose a high school, I decided to attend a technical high school. These schools offered subjects like computer programming and web development, which I thought would help me build a foundation for a future in cybersecurity. When I found out that I had been accepted into the technical school I wanted, I was thrilled. It felt like the first real step toward my career goals.

## A Turning Point: My First Official Cybersecurity Training

Around the time I was starting high school, I heard about a state-sponsored cybersecurity camp — the first of its kind in Turkey. The camp, called *81 İlde, 81 Hackers*, was aimed at university students, but I was determined to participate despite my young age. I reached out to the local university hosting the camp, explained my situation, and to my delight, they allowed me to join.

This was a huge milestone for me. Instead of learning from anonymous forum members, I would now be receiving training from real cybersecurity professionals. The camp took place at the same time as my first days of high school, but I was able to get special permission from the university to attend. This experience marked my true entry into the world of cybersecurity, and it significantly expanded my knowledge of web security. I realized then that I was on the right path.

## Discovering Bug Bounty Hunting

As I neared the end of high school, I discovered a new area within cybersecurity — bug bounty hunting. This concept intrigued me because it offered a way to apply the knowledge I had gained while also advancing in the field. After doing some research, I created accounts on platforms like HackerOne and Bugcrowd. However, I couldn't dedicate much time to bug bounty hunting at that point because I was focused on preparing for the university entrance exam.

When I finally took the exam and got accepted into a computer programming program, I felt like I was moving in the right direction. As I began my university studies, I also started diving deeper into bug bounty hunting, researching how it worked and how to get started. This period was all about preparation — laying the groundwork for the practical experience I was about to gain.

## First Bounties and Growing Motivation

My first real bug bounty experience didn't happen through an official platform. Instead, I used Google dorking to find a vulnerability in an e-commerce company's website. I discovered four different security issues: SQL Injection, XSS, SSRF, and CSRF. The company rewarded me for my findings, which motivated me even more. This initial success made me feel like I was truly making progress.

Later, I began hunting on HackerOne, but my early reports were often marked as informative, duplicate, or not acceptable. It was frustrating at times, but I didn't let it discourage me. I kept pushing forward. Eventually, I found my first valid vulnerability and earned a $150 bounty. That small victory showed me that I was on the right track.

Not long after, I discovered a critical vulnerability in the same program, and this time I was rewarded with $1,500. These successes not only boosted my confidence but also solidified my determination to continue advancing in this field.

## Looking Ahead: A Full-Time Focus

After a period of focusing more on university studies, I'm now ready to return to bug bounty hunting full-time. I've decided to fully commit to a career in cybersecurity and bug bounty hunting. My experiences have shown me that there's always more to learn, and I'm eager to keep developing my skills and reaching new heights in this field. For me, this is just the beginning.
